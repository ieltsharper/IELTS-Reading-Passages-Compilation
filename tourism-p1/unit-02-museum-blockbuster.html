Museum Blockbuster

A Since the 1980s, the term "blockbuster" has become the fashionable word for special spectacular museum, art gallery or science centre exhibitions. These exhibitions have the ability to attract large crowds and often large corporate sponsors. Here is one of some existing definitions of blockbuster: Put by Elsen (1984), a blockbuster is a "... large scale loan exhibition that people who normally don't go to museums will stand in line for hours to see ..." James Rosenfield, writing in Direct Marketing in 1993, has described a successful blockbuster exhibition as a "... triumph of both curatorial and marketing skills ..." My own definition for blockbuster is "a popular, high profile exhibition on display for a limited period, that attracts the general public, who are prepared to both stand in line and pay a fee in order to partake in the exhibition." What both Elsen and Rosenfield omit in their descriptions of blockbuster, is that people are prepared to pay a fee to see a blockbuster, and that the term blockbuster can just as easily apply to a movie or a museum exhibition.

B Merely naming an exhibition or movie a blockbuster however, does not make it a blockbuster. The term can only apply when the item in question has had an overwhelmingly successful response from the public. However, in literature from both the UK and USA the other words that also start to appear in descriptions of blockbuster are "less scholarly", "non-elitist" and "popularist". Detractors argue that blockbusters are designed to appeal to the lowest common denominator, while others extol the virtues of encouraging scholars to cooperate on projects, and to provide exhibitions that cater for a broad selection of the community rather than an elite sector.

C Maintaining and increasing visitor levels is paramount in the new museology. This requires continued product development. Not only the creation or hiring of blockbuster exhibitions, but regular exhibition changes and innovations. In addition, the visiting publics have become customers rather than visitors, and the skills that are valued in museums, science centres and galleries to keep the new customers coming through the door have changed. High on the list of requirements are commercial, business, marketing and entrepreneurial skills. Curators are now administrators. Being a director of an art gallery no longer requires an Art Degree. As succinctly summarised in the Economist in 1994 "business nous and public relation skills" were essential requirements for a director, and the ability to compete with other museums to stage travelling exhibitions which draw huge crowds.

D The new museology has resulted in the convergence of museums, the heritage industry, and tourism, profit-making and pleasure-giving. This has given rise to much debate about the appropriateness of adapting the activities of institutions so that they more closely reflect the priorities of the market place and whether it is appropriate to see museums primarily as tourist attractions. At many institutions you can now hold office functions in the display areas, or have dinner with the dinosaurs. Whatever commentators may think, managers of museums, art galleries and science centres worldwide are looking for artful ways to blend culture and commerce, and blockbuster exhibitions are at the top of the list. But while blockbusters are all part of the new museology, there is proof that you don't need a museum, science centre or art gallery to benefit from the drawing power of a blockbuster or to stage a blockbuster.

E But do blockbusters held in public institutions really create a surplus to fund other activities? If the bottom line is profit, then according to the accounting records of many major museums and galleries, blockbusters do make money. For some museums overseas, it may be the money that they need to update parts of their collections or to repair buildings that are in need of attention. For others in Australia, it may be the opportunity to illustrate that they are attempting to pay their way, by recovering part of their operating costs, or funding other operating activities with off-budget revenue. This makes the economic rationalists cheerful. However, not all exhibitions that are hailed to be blockbusters will be blockbusters, and some will not make money. It is also unlikely that the accounting systems of most institutions will recognise the real cost of either creating or hiring a blockbuster.

F Blockbusters require large capital expenditure, and draw on resources across all branches of an organisation; however, the costs don't end there. There is a Human Resource Management cost in addition to a measurable 'real' dollar cost. Receiving a touring exhibition involves large expenditure as well, and draws resources from across functional management structures in project management style. Everyone from a general labourer to a building servicing unit, the front of house, technical, promotion, education and administration staff, are required to perform additional tasks. Furthermore, as an increasing number of institutions in Australia try their hand at increasing visitor numbers, memberships (and therefore revenue), by staging blockbuster exhibitions, it may be less likely that blockbusters will continue to provide a surplus to subsidise other activities due to the competitive nature of the market. There are only so many consumer dollars to go around, and visitors will need to choose between blockbuster products.

G Unfortunately, when the bottom-line is the most important objective to the mounting of blockbuster exhibitions, this same objective can be hard to maintain. Creating, mounting or hiring blockbusters is exhausting for staff, with the real costs throughout an institution difficult to calculate. Although the direct aims may be financial, creating or hiring a blockbuster has many positive spin-offs; by raising their profile through a popular blockbuster exhibition, a museum will be seen in a more favorable light at budget time. Blockbusters mean crowds, and crowds are good for the local economy, providing increased employment for shops, hotels, restaurants, the transport industry and retailers. Blockbusters expose staff to the vagaries and pressures of the market place, and may lead to creative excellence. Either the success or failure of a blockbuster may highlight the need for managers and policy makers to rethink their strategies. However, the new museology and the apparent trend towards blockbusters make it likely that museums, art galleries and particularly science centres will be seen as part of the entertainment and tourism industry, rather than as cultural icons deserving of government and philanthropic support.

H Perhaps the best pathway to take is one that balances both blockbusters and regular exhibitions. However, this easy middle ground may only work if you have enough space, and have alternate sources of funding to continue to support the regular less exciting fare. Perhaps the advice should be to make sure that your regular activities and exhibitions are more enticing, and find out what your local community wants from you. The question (trend) now at most museums and science centres, is "What blockbusters can we tour to overseas venues and will it be cost effective?"

→ Xương sống: Có bằng chứng rằng không cần là bảo tàng hay phòng tranh vẫn tận dụng được sức hút của blockbuster.

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Bảo tàng học mới → hội tụ bảo tàng–du lịch–lợi nhuận → tranh luận → blockbuster là cách pha trộn văn hoá và thương mại.

✅ Câu 7 → museology (⟦7⟧ has contributed to the linking of museums, the heritage industry, tourism, profit-making and pleasure-giving.)

Dẫn chứng: “The new museology has resulted in the convergence of museums, the heritage industry, and tourism, profit-making and pleasure-giving.”

Giải thích: contributed to the linking = resulted in the convergence → museology (đáp án chính thức: museology / (the) new museology, đều ≤ 3 từ).

✅ Câu 8 → tourist attractions (There occurs some controversy over whether it is proper to consider museums mainly as ⟦8⟧)

Dẫn chứng: “much debate about … whether it is appropriate to see museums primarily as tourist attractions.”

Giải thích: controversy = debate; proper … mainly = appropriate … primarily → tourist attractions (2 từ).

Tiến độ sau Đoạn D: Xong câu 1–8. Còn: 9–13 (cần đoạn E–G).

ĐOẠN E

(1) But do blockbusters held in public institutions really create a surplus to fund other activities?

→ Nhưng liệu blockbuster ở các tổ chức công có thực sự tạo ra thặng dư để tài trợ hoạt động khác?

(2) If the bottom line is profit, then according to the accounting records of many major museums and galleries, blockbusters do make money.

→ Xương sống: Nếu xét lợi nhuận thì theo sổ sách nhiều bảo tàng lớn, blockbuster có sinh lời.

(3) For some museums overseas, it may be the money that they need to update parts of their collections or to repair buildings that are in need of attention.

→ Xương sống: Với một số bảo tàng nước ngoài, đó có thể là tiền cần để cập nhật bộ sưu tập hoặc sửa chữa toà nhà → câu 9–10 (lựa chọn A).

(4) For others in Australia, it may be the opportunity to illustrate that they are attempting to pay their way, by recovering part of their operating costs, or funding other operating activities with off-budget revenue (doanh thu).

→ Với bảo tàng Úc, đó là cơ hội chứng tỏ họ đang tự trang trải một phần chi phí.

(5) This makes the economic rationalists cheerful.

→ Điều này làm các nhà duy lý kinh tế vui vẻ.

(6) However, not all exhibitions that are hailed to be blockbusters will be blockbusters, and some will not make money.

→ Xương sống: Tuy nhiên không phải triển lãm nào được gọi là blockbuster cũng đúng nghĩa; một số không sinh lời.

(7) It is also unlikely that the accounting systems of most institutions will recognise the real cost of either creating or hiring a blockbuster.

→ Hệ thống kế toán cũng khó phản ánh chi phí thật của việc tạo/thuê blockbuster.

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Blockbuster có sinh lời → tiền sửa chữa/cập nhật → nhưng không phải cái nào cũng lời, chi phí thật bị che giấu.

⏸ Câu 9–10: Ở E3 xuất hiện lợi ích A (tiền cập nhật bộ sưu tập, sửa toà nhà). Cần thêm lợi ích thứ hai (đoạn G).

Tiến độ sau Đoạn E: Còn: 9–13.

ĐOẠN F

(1) Blockbusters require large capital expenditure, and draw on resources across all branches of an organisation; however, the costs don't end there.

→ Xương sống: Blockbuster đòi hỏi chi tiêu vốn lớn và huy động nguồn lực toàn tổ chức; tuy nhiên chi phí không dừng ở đó → câu 11–13 (lựa chọn E).

(2) There is a Human Resource Management cost in addition to a measurable `real' dollar cost.

→ Ngoài chi phí tiền thật còn có chi phí quản lý nhân sự.

(3) Receiving a touring exhibition involves large expenditure as well, and draws resources from across functional management structures in project management style.

→ Xương sống: Đón một triển lãm lưu động cũng tốn kém và huy động nguồn lực từ nhiều bộ phận quản lý.

(4) Everyone from a general labourer to a building servicing unit, the front of house, technical, promotion, education and administration staff, are required to perform additional tasks.

→ Xương sống: Từ lao động phổ thông đến bộ phận kỹ thuật, quảng bá, giáo dục, hành chính, ai cũng bị yêu cầu làm thêm việc → câu 11–13 (lựa chọn D).

💡 Chủ ngữ rất dài everyone from … to …, động từ are required ở cuối.

(5) Furthermore, as an increasing number of institutions in Australia try their hand at increasing visitor numbers, memberships (and therefore revenue), by staging blockbuster exhibitions, it may be less likely that blockbusters will continue to provide a surplus to subsidise other activities due to the competitive nature of the market.

→ Xương sống: Khi ngày càng nhiều tổ chức làm blockbuster, khó có khả năng chúng tiếp tục tạo thặng dư vì thị trường cạnh tranh.

(6) There are only so many consumer dollars to go around, and visitors will need to choose between blockbuster products.

→ Số tiền người tiêu dùng có hạn, nên khách phải chọn giữa các sản phẩm blockbuster → liên quan lựa chọn A (nhưng đề nói “hesitated”, không có trong bài).

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Chi phí vốn lớn → chi phí nhân sự → mọi nhân viên làm thêm việc → cạnh tranh làm giảm thặng dư.

⏸ Câu 11–13: Đoạn F có hai ứng viên: E (large capital expenditure, nhưng bài không nhắc “specialists”) và D (general labourer … additional tasks, gần như nguyên văn). Chờ đoạn G.

Tiến độ sau Đoạn F: Còn: 9–13.

ĐOẠN G

(1) Unfortunately, when the bottom-line is the most important objective (mục tiêu) to the mounting of blockbuster exhibitions, this same objective can be hard to maintain.

→ Xương sống: Khi lợi nhuận là mục tiêu số một thì mục tiêu đó khó duy trì.

(2) Creating, mounting or hiring blockbusters is exhausting for staff, with the real costs throughout an institution difficult to calculate.

→ Xương sống: Tạo, dàn dựng, thuê blockbuster làm nhân viên kiệt sức; chi phí thật khó tính → câu 11–13 (lựa chọn B).

(3) Although the direct aims may be financial, creating or hiring a blockbuster has many positive (tích cực) spin-offs; by raising their profile through a popular blockbuster exhibition, a museum will be seen in a more favorable light at budget time.

→ Xương sống: Dù mục tiêu trực tiếp là tài chính, blockbuster có nhiều lợi ích phụ (nâng uy tín khi xét ngân sách).

💡 Ý chính sau Although: lợi ích phụ.

(4) Blockbusters mean crowds, and crowds are good for the local economy (kinh tế địa phương), providing increased employment for shops, hotels, restaurants, the transport industry and retailers.

→ Xương sống: Đám đông tốt cho kinh tế địa phương, tạo thêm việc làm cho cửa hàng, khách sạn, giao thông → câu 10 (lựa chọn D = lợi ích).

(5) Blockbusters expose staff to the vagaries and pressures of the market place, and may lead to creative excellence.

→ Xương sống: Blockbuster đưa nhân viên vào áp lực thị trường nhưng cũng có thể dẫn đến sáng tạo xuất sắc (lựa chọn F – vừa tiêu cực vừa tích cực).

(6) Either the success or failure of a blockbuster may highlight the need for managers and policy makers to rethink their strategies.

→ Thành công hay thất bại đều có thể buộc nhà quản lý nghĩ lại chiến lược.

(7) However, the new museology and the apparent trend (xu hướng) towards blockbusters make it likely that museums, art galleries and particularly science centres will be seen as part of the entertainment and tourism industry, rather than as cultural icons deserving of government and philanthropic support.

→ Xương sống: Xu hướng này khiến bảo tàng bị xem như một phần ngành giải trí–du lịch, thay vì biểu tượng văn hoá đáng được hỗ trợ → câu 11–13 (lựa chọn C).

💡 However = mặt trái: mất vị thế văn hoá.

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Mục tiêu lợi nhuận khó giữ → nhân viên kiệt sức → lợi ích phụ (uy tín, kinh tế địa phương) → áp lực thị trường → bảo tàng bị coi như giải trí.

✅ Câu 9–10 → A, D (Which TWO advantages are mentioned by the writer of the text?)

Dẫn chứng: A – “the money that they need to update parts of their collections or to repair buildings”; D – “crowds are good for the local economy, providing increased employment for shops, hotels, restaurants …”

Giải thích: sufficient money to repair architectures = tiền sửa toà nhà; beneficial for the development of local business = tốt cho kinh tế địa phương/việc làm → A, D.

Bẫy: B – “maintaining and increasing visitor levels” là mục tiêu của bảo tàng (đoạn C), bài không nói blockbuster duy trì được lượng khách (đoạn F còn cho rằng thặng dư có thể giảm). C – “blend culture and commerce” là điều các nhà quản lý tìm kiếm, không phải lợi ích. E – không có bằng chứng.

✅ Câu 11–13 → B, C, E (Which THREE disadvantages are mentioned by the writer of the text?)

Dẫn chứng: B – “Creating, mounting or hiring blockbusters is exhausting for staff”; C – “seen as part of the entertainment and tourism industry, rather than as cultural icons”; E – “Blockbusters require large capital expenditure”.

Giải thích: Workers has become tired of workloads = exhausting for staff; content more entertaining rather than cultural = entertainment … rather than cultural icons; huge amounts of capital = large capital expenditure.

Bẫy: ⚠ Câu gây tranh cãi (đáp án chính thức giữ B, C, E): D (general labourer … additional tasks) được nêu gần nguyên văn ở đoạn F, và F (expose staff to pressures of the market place) xuất hiện ở đoạn G; trong khi E nói “invested in specialists” còn bài chỉ nói “large capital expenditure”. Giáo viên cần quyết định: giữ B, C, E hay đổi E → D. A bị loại: bài không nói khách “do dự chọn triển lãm”.
