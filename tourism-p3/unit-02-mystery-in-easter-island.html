Mystery in Easter Island!

A One of the world's most famous yet least visited archaeological sites, Easter Island is a small, hilly, now treeless island of volcanic origin. Located in the Pacific Ocean at 27 degrees south of the equator and some 2200 miles (3600 kilometers) off the coast of Chile, it is considered to be the world's most remote inhabited island. The island is, technically speaking, a single massive volcano rising over ten thousand feet from the Pacific Ocean floor. The island received its most well-known current name, Easter Island, from the Dutch sea captain Jacob Roggeveen who became the first European to visit Easter Sunday, April 5, 1722.

B In the early 1950s, the Norwegian explorer Thor Heyerdahl popularized the idea that the island had been originally settled by advanced societies of Indians from the coast of South America. Extensive archaeological, ethnographic and linguistic research has conclusively shown this hypothesis to be inaccurate. It is now recognized that the original inhabitants of Easter Island are of Polynesian stock (DNA extracts from skeletons have confirmed this), that they most probably came from the Marquesas or Society islands, and that they arrived as early as 318 AD (carbon dating of reeds from a grave confirms this). At the time of their arrival, much of the island was forested, was teeming with land birds, and was perhaps the most productive breeding site for seabirds in the Polynesia region. Because of the plentiful bird, fish and plant food sources, the human population grew and gave rise to a rich religious and artistic culture.

C That culture's most famous features are its enormous stone statues called moai, at least 288 of which one stood upon massive stone platforms called ahu. There are some 250 of these ahu platforms spaced approximately one half mile apart and creating an almost unbroken line around the perimeter of the island. Another 600 moai statues, in various stages of completion, are scattered around the island, either in quarries or along ancient roads between the quarries and the coastal areas where the statues were most often erected. Nearly all the moai are carved from the tough stone of the Rano Raraku volcano. The average statue is 14 feet and 6 inches tall and weighs 14 tons. Some moai were as large as 33 feet and weighed more than 80 tons. Depending upon the size of the statues, it has been estimated that between 50 and 150 people were needed to drag them across the countryside on sleds and rollers made from the island's trees.

D Scholars are unable to definitively explain the function and use of the moai statues. It is assumed that their carving and erection derived from an idea rooted in similar practices found elsewhere in Polynesia but which evolved in a unique way on Easter Island. Archaeological and iconographic analysis indicates that the statue cult was based on an ideology of male, lineage-based authority incorporating anthropomorphic symbolism. The statues were thus symbols of authority and power, both religious and political. But they were not only symbols. To the people who erected and used them, they were actual repositories of sacred spirit. Carved stone and wooden objects in ancient Polynesian religions, when properly fashioned and ritually prepared, were believed to be charged by a magical spiritual essence called mana. The ahu platforms of Easter Island were the sanctuaries of the people, and the moai statues were the ritually charged sacred objects of those sanctuaries.

E Besides its more well-known name, Easter Island is also known as Te-Pito-O-Te-Henua, meaning `The Navel of the World', and as Mata-Ki-Te-Rani, meaning `Eyes Looking at Heaven'. These ancient name and a host of mythological details ignored by mainstream archaeologists, point to the possibility that the remote island may once have been a geodetic marker and the site of an astronomical observatory of a long forgotten civilization. In his book, Heaven's Mirror, Graham Hancock suggests that Easter Island may once have been a significant scientific outpost of this antediluvian civilization and that its location had extreme importance in a planet-spanning, mathematically precise grid of sacred sites. Two other alternative scholars, Christopher Knight and Robert Lomas, have extensively studied the location and possible function of these geodetic markers. In their fascinating book, Uriel's Machine, they suggest that one purpose of the geodetic markers was as part of global network of sophisticated astronomical observatories dedicated to predicting and preparing for future commentary impacts and crystal displacement cataclysms.

F In the latter years of the 20th century and the first years of the 21st century various writers and scientists have advanced theories regarding the rapid decline of Easter Island's magnificent civilization around the time of the first European contact. Principal among these theories, and now shown to be inaccurate, is that postulated by Jared Diamond in his book Collapse: How Societies Choose to Fail or Survive. Basically these theories state that a few centuries after Easter Island's initial colonization the resource needs of the growing population had begun to outpace the island's capacity to renew itself ecologically. By the 1400s the forests had been entirely cut, the rich ground cover had eroded away, the springs had dried up, and the vast flocks of birds coming to roost on the island had disappeared. With no logs to build canoes for offshore fishing, with depleted bird and wildlife food sources, and with declining crop yields because of the erosion of good soil, the nutritional intake of the people plummeted. First famine, then cannibalism, set in. Because the island could no longer feed the chiefs, bureaucrats and priests who kept the complex society running, the resulting chaos triggered a social and cultural collapse. By 1700 the population dropped to between one-quarter and one-tenth of its former number, and many of the statues were toppled during supposed "clan wars" of the 1600 and 1700s.

G The faulty notions presented in these theories began with the racist assumptions of Thor Heyerdahl and have been perpetuated by writers, such as Jared Diamond, who do not have sufficient archaeological and historical understanding of the actual events which occurred on Easter Island. The real truth regarding the tremendous social devastation which occurred on Easter Island is that it was a direct consequence of the inhumane behavior of many of the first European visitors, particularly the slavers who raped and murdered the islanders, introduced small pox and other diseases, and brutally removed the natives to mainland South America.

→ Xương sống: Các học giả không thể giải thích dứt khoát chức năng và công dụng của moai.

(2) It is assumed that their carving and erection derived from an idea rooted in similar (tương tự) practices found elsewhere in Polynesia but which evolved in a unique (độc đáo) way on Easter Island.

→ Xương sống: Người ta cho rằng việc đẽo và dựng tượng bắt nguồn từ ý tưởng giống các tập tục ở nơi khác tại Polynesia, nhưng phát triển theo cách độc đáo ở Đảo Phục Sinh → câu 33.

(3) Archaeological and iconographic analysis (phân tích) indicates that the statue cult was based on an ideology (hệ tư tưởng) of male, lineage-based authority (quyền lực) incorporating anthropomorphic symbolism.

→ Xương sống: Phân tích cho thấy tục thờ tượng dựa trên hệ tư tưởng quyền lực của nam giới theo dòng dõi.

(4) The statues were thus symbols of authority and power, both religious and political.

→ Xương sống: Tượng là biểu tượng của quyền lực, cả tôn giáo lẫn chính trị.

(5) But they were not only symbols.

→ Xương sống: Nhưng chúng không chỉ là biểu tượng → bước ngoặt ý (câu 28).

(6) To the people who erected and used them, they were actual repositories of sacred spirit.

→ Với người dựng và dùng tượng, chúng là nơi chứa linh hồn thiêng.

(7) Carved stone and wooden objects in ancient Polynesian religions, when properly fashioned and ritually prepared, were believed to be charged by a magical spiritual essence called mana.

→ Đồ đá, gỗ chạm khắc trong tôn giáo Polynesia cổ, khi được chế tác đúng, được tin là chứa năng lượng thiêng “mana”.

(8) The ahu platforms of Easter Island were the sanctuaries of the people, and the moai statues were the ritually charged sacred objects of those sanctuaries.

→ Bệ ahu là nơi thờ, moai là vật thiêng trong nơi thờ đó.

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Chức năng chưa rõ → bắt nguồn Polynesia nhưng phát triển riêng → biểu tượng quyền lực + vật thiêng.

✅ Câu 28 → ii (Paragraph D)

Dẫn chứng: “The statues were thus symbols of authority and power … But they were not only symbols. … actual repositories of sacred spirit.”

Giải thích: symbols of combined purposes = tượng vừa là biểu tượng quyền lực (tôn giáo + chính trị) vừa là vật chứa linh thiêng → ii.

Bẫy: ix (Symbols of authority and power) chỉ khớp một nửa đoạn, bỏ mất ý “not only symbols … sacred spirit”; ⚠ ranh giới ii/ix khá mong manh, giữ đáp án chính thức ii.

✅ Câu 33 → FALSE (The Moai can be found not only on Easter Island but also elsewhere in Polynesia.)

Dẫn chứng: “an idea rooted in similar practices found elsewhere in Polynesia but which evolved in a unique way on Easter Island”

Giải thích: bài nói tập tục tương tự (không phải tượng moai) tồn tại ở nơi khác; moai là sản phẩm độc đáo của Đảo Phục Sinh → câu đề (moai cũng có ở nơi khác) trái với bài → FALSE.

Bẫy: ⚠ Cần giáo viên xem lại: bài không nói thẳng “moai chỉ có trên Easter Island”; kết luận FALSE được suy ra từ “unique way”. Có thể bị coi là NOT GIVEN; giữ FALSE theo key chính thức.

Tiến độ sau Đoạn D: Xong câu 27, 28, 31–33. Còn: 29, 30, 34–40.

ĐOẠN E (câu 29, 34, 35)

(1) Besides its more well-known name, Easter Island is also known as Te-Pito-O-Te-Henua, meaning ‘The Navel of the World’, and as Mata-Ki-Te-Rani, meaning ‘Eyes Looking at Heaven’.

→ Xương sống: Ngoài tên quen thuộc, đảo còn có hai tên cổ: “Rốn của Thế giới” và “Đôi Mắt Nhìn Lên Trời”.

(2) These ancient name and a host of mythological details ignored by mainstream archaeologists, point to the possibility that the remote island may once have been a geodetic marker and the site of an astronomical observatory of a long forgotten civilization.

→ Xương sống: Các tên cổ và chi tiết thần thoại bị các nhà khảo cổ chính thống phớt lờ gợi ý đảo từng là mốc trắc địa và đài thiên văn của một nền văn minh bị lãng quên → câu 29, 34.

(3) In his book, Heaven's Mirror, Graham Hancock suggests that Easter Island may once have been a significant (quan trọng) scientific outpost of this antediluvian civilization and that its location (vị trí) had extreme importance in a planet-spanning, mathematically precise (chính xác) grid of sacred sites.

→ Xương sống: Graham Hancock cho rằng đảo từng là tiền đồn khoa học của nền văn minh cổ, vị trí có tầm quan trọng trong lưới các địa điểm thiêng chính xác về toán học.

(4) Two other alternative (khác biệt) scholars, Christopher Knight and Robert Lomas, have extensively studied the location and possible function (chức năng) of these geodetic markers.

→ Xương sống: Hai học giả “thay thế” khác nghiên cứu kỹ vị trí và chức năng của các mốc trắc địa.

(5) In their fascinating book, Uriel's Machine, they suggest that one purpose of the geodetic markers was as part of global network (mạng lưới toàn cầu) of sophisticated astronomical observatories dedicated to predicting and preparing for future commentary impacts and crystal displacement cataclysms.

→ Xương sống: Họ cho rằng các mốc là một phần mạng lưới đài thiên văn toàn cầu nhằm dự báo và chuẩn bị cho các tác động từ thiên thạch/sao chổi và thảm họa địa chất.

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Tên cổ + thần thoại → đảo có thể là mốc trắc địa/đài thiên văn → Hancock, Knight & Lomas.

✅ Câu 29 → iii (Paragraph E)

Dẫn chứng: “the remote island may once have been a geodetic marker and the site of an astronomical observatory … a significant scientific outpost … astronomical observatories”

Giải thích: ancient spots which indicate scientific application = địa điểm cổ (mốc trắc địa, đài thiên văn) có chức năng khoa học → iii.

Bẫy: iv (The story of the name) và x (The Navel of the World) chỉ khớp câu 1 (tên đảo); phần chính của đoạn nói về các giả thuyết khoa học.

✅ Câu 34 → FALSE (Most archeologists recognised the religious and astronomical functions for an ancient society.)

Dẫn chứng: “a host of mythological details ignored by mainstream archaeologists, point to the possibility that the remote island may once have been … the site of an astronomical observatory”

Giải thích: Most archeologists = mainstream archaeologists; họ phớt lờ (ignored) khả năng này → nghĩa ngược với recognised → FALSE.

Bẫy: đoạn D nói học giả công nhận chức năng tôn giáo/chính trị, nhưng chức năng thiên văn thì bị bỏ qua → cả câu không đúng.

✅ Câu 35 → NOT GIVEN (The structures of Easter Island work as an astronomical outpost for extraterrestrial visitors.)

Dẫn chứng: bài chỉ nói “a significant scientific outpost of this antediluvian civilization”, “observatories dedicated to predicting … cometary impacts”

Giải thích: có “outpost / observatory” nhưng của nền văn minh cổ (antediluvian), mục đích dự báo thiên thạch; không hề nhắc người ngoài hành tinh (extraterrestrial visitors) → NOT GIVEN.

Tiến độ sau Đoạn E: Xong câu 27–29, 31–35. Còn: 30, 36–40.

ĐOẠN F (câu 36, 37)

(1) In the latter years of the 20th century and the first years of the 21st century various writers and scientists have advanced theories regarding the rapid decline (suy tàn) of Easter Island's magnificent civilization around the time of the first European contact (tiếp xúc).

→ Xương sống: Cuối TK20, đầu TK21, nhiều tác giả đưa ra các thuyết về sự suy tàn nhanh của nền văn minh khi người châu Âu đến.

(2) Principal (chính) among these theories, and now shown to be inaccurate, is that postulated by Jared Diamond in his book Collapse: How Societies Choose to Fail or Survive.

→ Xương sống: Thuyết chính (của Jared Diamond), nay đã được chứng minh là sai → câu 36.

(3) Basically these theories state that a few centuries after Easter Island's initial (ban đầu) colonization the resource needs of the growing population had begun to outpace the island's capacity (khả năng) to renew itself ecologically.

→ Xương sống: Các thuyết này nói nhu cầu tài nguyên của dân số ngày càng tăng bắt đầu vượt quá khả năng tự phục hồi của đảo → câu 37.

(4) By the 1400s the forests had been entirely cut, the rich ground cover had eroded away, the springs had dried up, and the vast flocks of birds coming to roost on the island had disappeared.

→ Đến 1400s rừng bị chặt hết, đất mặt xói mòn, suối cạn, chim biến mất.

(5) With no logs to build canoes for offshore fishing, with depleted bird and wildlife food sources, and with declining crop yields because of the erosion of good soil, the nutritional intake of the people plummeted.

→ Không còn gỗ đóng thuyền, nguồn thức ăn cạn, mùa màng giảm → dinh dưỡng của người dân sụt mạnh.

(6) First famine, then cannibalism, set in.

→ Trước hết là nạn đói, rồi đến ăn thịt người.

(7) Because the island could no longer feed the chiefs, bureaucrats and priests who kept the complex (phức tạp) society running, the resulting chaos triggered a social and cultural collapse (sụp đổ).

→ Đảo không nuôi nổi tầng lớp cai trị → hỗn loạn gây ra sự sụp đổ xã hội, văn hóa.

(8) By 1700 the population dropped to between one-quarter and one-tenth of its former number, and many of the statues were toppled during supposed “clan wars” of the 1600 and 1700s.

→ Đến 1700 dân số chỉ còn 1/4–1/10; nhiều tượng bị lật đổ trong các “cuộc chiến bộ tộc”.

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Thuyết Diamond: dân số tăng → cạn kiệt tài nguyên → đói, ăn thịt người → sụp đổ (và bị bác bỏ).

✅ Câu 36 → TRUE (the theory that depleted natural resources leading to the fail of Easter Island actual has a distorted perspective)

Dẫn chứng: “Principal among these theories, and now shown to be inaccurate, is that postulated by Jared Diamond …” (và đoạn G: “The faulty notions presented in these theories …”)

Giải thích: distorted perspective = inaccurate / faulty notions; depleted natural resources leading to the fail = thuyết cạn kiệt tài nguyên → sụp đổ → TRUE.

✅ Câu 37 → growing population (Some say the resources are depleted by a ⟦37⟧)

Dẫn chứng: “the resource needs of the growing population had begun to outpace the island's capacity to renew itself ecologically”

Giải thích: resources are depleted by … = resource needs … outpace capacity; chỗ trống là nguyên nhân → growing population (2 từ).

Tiến độ sau Đoạn F: Xong câu 27–29, 31–37. Còn: 30, 38–40.

ĐOẠN G (câu 30, 38–40)

(1) The faulty notions presented in these theories began with the racist assumptions of Thor Heyerdahl and have been perpetuated by writers, such as Jared Diamond, who do not have sufficient (đủ) archaeological and historical understanding of the actual events which occurred on Easter Island.

→ Xương sống: Những quan niệm sai này bắt đầu từ những giả định phân biệt chủng tộc của Heyerdahl và được các tác giả như Diamond tiếp nối vì không đủ hiểu biết khảo cổ và lịch sử → câu 38, 39.

(2) The real truth regarding the tremendous social devastation which occurred on Easter Island is that it was a direct consequence of the inhumane behavior of many of the first European visitors, particularly the slavers who raped and murdered the islanders, introduced small pox and other diseases, and brutally removed the natives to mainland South America.

→ Xương sống: Sự thật là tàn phá xã hội do hành vi vô nhân đạo của những người châu Âu đầu tiên, nhất là bọn buôn nô lệ (giết người, đem bệnh đậu mùa, bắt dân đi) → câu 40.

💡 Đoạn G bác bỏ các thuyết ở F và đưa cách giải thích đối lập.

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Quan niệm sai → bắt nguồn từ định kiến của Heyerdahl, Diamond thiếu hiểu biết → sự thật: người châu Âu gây ra.

✅ Câu 30 → viii (Paragraph G)

Giải thích: countering explanation to the misconceptions = real truth đối lập faulty notions; politically manipulated = racist assumptions → viii.
