Changes to the Soviet Union’s Working Week

27 ……………………………Heading question 27 for the next paragraph

The Soviet Union's first Five Year Plan was intended to turn the weak and undeveloped country into a powerful, modern, industrial nation. Following its introduction in 1928, production of coal, iron and steel increased at a fantastic rate, and new industrial cities sprang up. Everyone's life was affected, as collectivised farming drove millions from the land to swell the industrial proletariat. Private enterprise disappeared in city and country, leaving the state supreme under the leadership of Stalin. Unlimited enthusiasm was the mood of the day, with the Communists believing that iron will and hard-working manpower alone would bring about a new world.

28 ……………………………Heading question 28 for the next paragraph

Not even time itself was immune to efforts to make the state a huge, efficient machine, where not a moment would be wasted, especially in the workplace. Stalin's predecessor Lenin had already been intrigued by the ideas of Taylor, the American management expert whose time and motion studies had discovered ways of streamlining effort so that every worker could produce the maximum. The Communists were also great admirers of Ford's assembly line mass production and of his tractors that were imported in their thousands. Emulating and surpassing such capitalist models formed part of the training of the new Soviet Man.

iii An attempt to create better working conditions?

The goals of Communism had always included improving the lives of the proletariat. One major step in that direction was the announcement in 1927 that reduced the working day from eight to seven hours, in January 1929, all industries were ordered to adopt the shorter day by the end of the Plan. However, the state took away more than it gave, for this was part of a scheme to increase production by establishing a three-shift system, for six days a week. That meant that the factories were open day and night, and many people had to work at highly undesirable hours.

29 ……………………………Heading question 29 for the next paragraph

Hardly had that policy been announced, though, than the economist Larin came up with an idea for even greater efficiency. Workers were free and plants were closed on Sundays. Why not abolish that wasted day by instituting a continuous working week so that the machines could operate to their full capacity every day of the week? Stalin approved, and the continuous working week was introduced in August 1929 with immediate effect. This was during the height of enthusiasm for the Plan, whose goals the new schedule seemed guaranteed to forward.

30 ……………………………Heading question 30 for the next paragraph

The idea turned out to be very complicated in practice. Obviously, the workers couldn't be made to work seven days a week, nor should their total work hours be increased. The solution was ingenious: a new five-day week would have the workers on the job for four days, with the fifth day free. Staggering the rest days between groups of workers meant that each worker would spend the same number of hours on the job, but the factories would be working a full 360 days a year instead of 300. Workers in each establishment were divided into five groups, each assigned a colour which appeared on the new calendars distributed all over the country. This colour- coding was a valuable device, since without it workers might have trouble remembering what their day off was going to be, as it would change every week.

31 ……………………………Heading question 31 for the next paragraph

Official propaganda stressed the material and cultural benefits of the new scheme. Workers would get more rest; production and employment would increase (more workers would be needed to keep the factories running continuously); the standard of living would improve. Shopping and leisure activities would no longer have to be crammed into a weekend, so facilities would be far less crowded. The only objection concerned the family, where normally more than one member was working: the government insisted that the narrow family was far less important than the vast common good and, besides, arrangements could be made for husband and wife to share a common schedule.

32 ……………………………Heading question 32 for the next paragraph

The continuous working week spread fast, reaching its peak in October 1930, when it affected nearly three quarters of workers. In fact, many managers simply claimed that their factories had gone over to the new week, without actually applying it. By then, though, problems were becoming obvious. The workers hated it. Coordination of family schedules was virtually impossible, so husbands and wives only saw each other before or after work. Confusion reigned: the new plan was implemented haphazardly, with some factories operating five-, six- and seven- day weeks at the same time, and the workers often not getting their rest days at all.

33 ……………………………Heading question 33 for the next paragraph

Furthermore, the new week was far from having the vaunted effect on production. With the complicated rotation system, the work teams found themselves doing different kinds of work in successive weeks. Machines, no longer consistently in the hands of people who knew how to tend them, were often poorly maintained. Workers lost a sense of responsibility for the tasks they had normally performed. As a result, the new week started to lose ground.

34 ……………………………Heading question 34 for the next paragraph

In November 1931, the government ordered the widespread adoption of a new six-day week, including one day off, which had its own calendar. By July 1935, only a quarter of workers still followed the continuous schedule, and the six-day week too was soon on its way out. Both were finally abandoned in 1940, as part of the general reversion to more traditional methods, and Sunday returned as the universal day of rest. The experiment had failed.

✅ Câu 30 – Đoạn 5 → vi (how the continuous working week was organised)

Dẫn chứng: “a new five-day week would have the workers on the job for four days… Staggering the rest days… Workers… were divided into five groups, each assigned a colour…”

Giải thích: Mô tả chi tiết cách vận hành tuần làm việc liên tục → vi.

✅ Câu 40 → colour-coding (hoặc: colour coding / colours)

Dẫn chứng: “each assigned a colour which appeared on the new calendars… This colour-coding was a valuable device”

Giải thích: Hệ thống phân biệt các nhóm trên lịch = colour-coding.

ĐOẠN 6 (câu 31)

(1) Official propaganda stressed the material and cultural (văn hóa) benefits (lợi ích) of the new scheme (kế hoạch).

→ Tuyên truyền chính thức nhấn mạnh lợi ích vật chất và văn hóa của chế độ mới (“pros”).

(2) Workers would get more rest; production and employment would increase (more workers would be needed to keep the factories running continuously); the standard of living would improve.

→ Công nhân sẽ được nghỉ nhiều hơn; sản lượng và việc làm sẽ tăng; mức sống sẽ cải thiện.

(3) Shopping and leisure activities would no longer have to be crammed into a weekend, so facilities would be far less crowded.

→ Mua sắm, giải trí không còn phải dồn vào cuối tuần, nên các cơ sở sẽ bớt đông đúc hơn nhiều.

(4) The only objection concerned the family, where normally more than one member was working: the government insisted that the narrow family was far less important than the vast common good and, besides, arrangements could be made for husband and wife to share a common schedule (lịch trình).

→ Xương sống 1: Phản đối duy nhất liên quan đến gia đình (thường có nhiều người cùng đi làm) → câu 37 (“cons”).

→ Xương sống 2: chính phủ khăng khăng rằng gia đình nhỏ kém quan trọng hơn lợi ích chung, và vợ chồng có thể được xếp cùng lịch.

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Lợi ích được tuyên truyền (nghỉ nhiều, việc làm, mức sống, mua sắm, giải trí) → điểm trừ duy nhất: gia đình → phản hồi của chính phủ.

✅ Câu 31 – Đoạn 6 → xi (Pros and cons of the continuous working week)

Dẫn chứng: “Official propaganda stressed the material and cultural benefits… The only objection concerned the family…”

Giải thích: Lợi ích (pros) + phản đối (cons) → xi.

✅ Câu 37 → A (family life)

Dẫn chứng: “The only objection concerned the family, where normally more than one member was working”

Giải thích: Gia đình là điều duy nhất bị lo ngại sẽ không được cải thiện → A. B, C, D (giải trí, việc làm, mua sắm) đều nằm trong danh sách lợi ích.

ĐOẠN 7 (câu 32)

(1) The continuous working week spread fast, reaching its peak in October 1930, when it affected (ảnh hưởng) nearly three quarters of workers.

→ Tuần làm việc liên tục lan rộng nhanh, đạt đỉnh tháng 10/1930, ảnh hưởng gần 3/4 công nhân.

(2) In fact, many managers simply claimed that their factories had gone over to the new week, without actually applying it.

→ Thực tế, nhiều quản đốc chỉ tuyên bố nhà máy đã chuyển sang tuần mới mà không thực sự áp dụng.

(3) By then, though, problems were becoming obvious (rõ ràng).

→ Tuy nhiên, đến lúc đó các vấn đề đã trở nên rõ ràng.

(4) The workers hated it.

→ Công nhân ghét nó.

(5) Coordination (Sự phối hợp) of family schedules (lịch trình) was virtually (gần như) impossible, so husbands and wives only saw each other before or after work.

→ Phối hợp lịch gia đình gần như bất khả thi, nên vợ chồng chỉ gặp nhau trước hoặc sau giờ làm.

(6) Confusion reigned: the new plan was implemented (triển khai) haphazardly, with some factories operating five-, six- and seven-day weeks at the same time, and the workers often not getting their rest days at all.

→ Hỗn loạn bao trùm: kế hoạch được triển khai tùy tiện, có nhà máy cùng lúc chạy tuần 5, 6, 7 ngày, và công nhân thường không được nghỉ.

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Lan rộng nhanh → nhưng nhiều nơi chỉ áp dụng trên giấy → vấn đề: công nhân ghét, gia đình xáo trộn, triển khai hỗn loạn, không đồng bộ.

✅ Câu 32 – Đoạn 7 → iv (not consistently introduced everywhere)

Dẫn chứng: “many managers simply claimed that their factories had gone over to the new week, without actually applying it… the new plan was implemented haphazardly, with some factories operating five-, six- and seven-day weeks at the same time”

Giải thích: Áp dụng tùy tiện, mỗi nơi một kiểu = không được triển khai nhất quán → iv.

ĐOẠN 8 (câu 33)

(1) Furthermore, the new week was far from having the vaunted effect on production.

→ Hơn nữa, tuần mới còn lâu mới đạt hiệu quả sản xuất như được ca ngợi.

(2) With the complicated rotation system, the work teams (đội) found themselves doing different kinds of work in successive (liên tiếp) weeks.

→ Với hệ thống luân phiên phức tạp, các tổ phải làm những việc khác nhau qua các tuần liên tiếp.

(3) Machines, no longer consistently (một cách nhất quán) in the hands of people who knew how to tend them, were often poorly maintained (bảo trì).

→ Máy móc, không còn nằm trong tay người biết vận hành, thường bị bảo trì kém.

(4) Workers lost a sense of responsibility for the tasks (nhiệm vụ) they had normally (thường lệ) performed.

→ Công nhân mất ý thức trách nhiệm với công việc vốn quen làm.

(5) As a result, the new week started to lose ground.

→ Kết quả là tuần mới bắt đầu mất chỗ đứng.

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Thất bại về sản xuất: luân phiên phức tạp → máy móc bị bảo trì kém → công nhân mất trách nhiệm → tuần mới suy yếu.

✅ Câu 33 – Đoạn 8 → x (the failure of factories to achieve expected improvements)

Dẫn chứng: “the new week was far from having the vaunted effect on production… Machines… were often poorly maintained.”

Giải thích: Không đạt hiệu quả sản xuất được kỳ vọng → x.

ĐOẠN 9 (câu 34)

(1) In November 1931, the government ordered the widespread (rộng rãi) adoption of a new six-day week, including one day off, which had its own calendar.

→ Tháng 11/1931, chính phủ ra lệnh áp dụng rộng rãi tuần 6 ngày mới (nghỉ 1 ngày), có lịch riêng.

(2) By July 1935, only a quarter of workers still followed the continuous schedule (lịch trình), and the six-day week too was soon on its way out.

→ Đến 7/1935, chỉ 1/4 công nhân còn theo lịch liên tục, và tuần 6 ngày cũng sớm bị loại bỏ.

(3) Both were finally abandoned (bị bãi bỏ) in 1940, as part of the general (chung) reversion to more traditional methods (phương pháp truyền thống), and Sunday returned as the universal day of rest.

→ Cả hai cuối cùng bị bãi bỏ năm 1940, trong xu hướng quay lại phương pháp truyền thống, và Chủ nhật trở lại là ngày nghỉ chung.

(4) The experiment had failed.

→ Cuộc thử nghiệm đã thất bại.

🔗 Mạch đoạn: Thay bằng tuần 6 ngày → cả hai dần bị bỏ → năm 1940 bãi bỏ hoàn toàn, Chủ nhật trở lại → kết luận: thất bại.

✅ Câu 34 – Đoạn 9 → ii (the end of two short-lived schemes)

Dẫn chứng: “Both were finally abandoned in 1940” (tuần liên tục 1929 và tuần 6 ngày 1931).

Giải thích: Hai chế độ tồn tại ngắn đều bị bãi bỏ → ii.

Heading thừa: v (success in replacing foreign goods), viii (a proposal by factory workers).
